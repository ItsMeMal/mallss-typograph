# Visual Modes and Motion Language

Use this as a palette, not a checklist to apply all at once.

## Modes

### Type-only
Best when words themselves carry emotional or narrative weight. Build expressive layouts using scale, rhythm, alignment, cropping, masking, tracking, and negative space. Avoid karaoke-like word highlighting unless explicitly requested.

### Photo-led
Best when the piece needs people, places, memories, objects, or a visual narrative. Sequence images with intent. Use crop changes, parallax, layered movement, cut-outs, match cuts, and controlled camera moves. Avoid random slideshow pacing.

### Mixed media
Best when typography and photographs can interact. Place type in front of, behind, around, or within imagery. Use layers, masking, panels, collage, depth, and shape continuity. Preserve legibility and subject integrity.

### Abstract / graphic
Best when music is instrumental, mood-led, or conceptual. Translate rhythm and timbre into shape, texture, movement, and spatial composition. Avoid generic waveform visualizers unless appropriate.

### Footage-led
Best when actual moving imagery supports the message. Use meaningful cuts, reframing, speed changes, match movement, and typography as a designed layer. Do not use footage merely to fill space.

## Motion families
- **Impact:** slam, snap, scale punch, directional shove. Reserve for accents.
- **Reveal:** mask wipe, type-on, crop reveal, tracking open, line-by-line reveal.
- **Flow:** drift, pan, push, pull, parallax, continuous camera movement.
- **Texture:** grain, displacement, restrained glitch, echo, smear, motion blur.
- **Transform:** shape morph, word-to-word transformation, rotation, perspective shift.
- **Pause:** hold, fade to negative space, still image, minimal detail.

Pair motion with meaning. Loud does not always mean fast; calm does not always mean static.

## Visual progression
A video should evolve. Plan changes in density, scale, color, image frequency, and motion intensity across sections. Use recurring motifs to maintain unity. Do not change every design variable at every cut.
